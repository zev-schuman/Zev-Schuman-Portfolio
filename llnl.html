Summer 2025 • Internship

Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory

Graduate Mechanical Engineering Intern • Telescope Satellite Project

Project Overview

During my summer internship at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, I designed and fabricated precision clamps for mounting telescope satellite lenses. This project demanded rigorous engineering analysis to meet NASA's General Environmental Verification Specification (GEVS) requirements for space flight.

Using principles of elastic averaging, I developed a solution that overcame design constraints while maintaining precision tolerances. Through extensive hand calculations and iterative prototyping, I created a design that the team is implementing on an upcoming satellite launch.

Design Challenge

The lens mounting system needed to meet all of NASA's GEVS requirements, including thermal cycling, vibration resistance, and dimensional stability. Traditional rigid mounting approaches failed to distribute loads effectively, leading me to explore elastic averaging principles.

Elastic Averaging Approach

Elastic averaging is a design principle that uses compliant interfaces to distribute loads across multiple contact points. By designing the clamp with carefully controlled flexibility, I was able to:

  • • Accommodate dimensional variations in the lens assembly
  • • Distribute clamping forces evenly across the lens surface
  • • Maintain optical alignment through extreme temperature cycles
  • • Isolate the lens from vibration during launch and operation

Design Process

Hand Calculations

I performed all calculations by hand, including stress analysis, thermal expansion, and load distribution. This deep analytical approach ensured I understood every aspect of the design before prototyping.

Iterative Prototyping

I built and tested multiple prototypes, refining the design based on assembly fit, load distribution, and performance under thermal cycling. Each iteration brought me closer to the optimal solution.

Materials Selection: RTV566

After evaluating several silicone-based elastomers, I selected RTV566 as the optimal adhesive for this application. RTV566 was chosen because it:

  • Withstands launch conditions: Maintains properties through extreme G-forces and vibration
  • Low CTE: Minimal thermal expansion across operating temperatures
  • Outgassing compliant: Meets NASA outgassing requirements for vacuum environments
  • Sufficient cushioning: Provides the compliance needed for elastic averaging

Testing & Validation

I designed and performed comprehensive tests to evaluate the RTV566 adhesive bonding strength under various conditions:

  • • Environmental stress testing (thermal cycling)
  • • Vibration exposure testing
  • • Static and dynamic load testing
  • • Outgassing verification

Additional Contributions

Beyond the clamp design, I contributed to the satellite assembly process through:

  • • Bolt torque calculations for all fasteners in the assembly
  • • Dynamic tracking spreadsheet to monitor bolt torque during assembly and ensure proper preload

Project Impact

At the conclusion of my internship, I presented my design recommendation to the team. Based on my analysis and testing results, they approved my design for implementation on an upcoming satellite launch. This validated both the technical soundness of the approach and my ability to deliver production-ready engineering solutions.

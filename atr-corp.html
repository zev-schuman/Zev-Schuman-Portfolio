Summer 2024 • Internship

UAV Launcher Design

Advanced Technology and Research Corp

Project Overview

During my summer internship at Advanced Technology and Research Corp in Beltsville, MD, I worked with an engineering team to design and build a UAV launcher system. This 1/8th scale model will eventually launch fighter jets, but currently launches remote control UAVs weighing approximately 30 lbs.

My contributions focused on analytical modeling, design iteration, and field validation through live testing at Griffon Aerospace in Huntsville, Alabama.

Launch Mechanism

The launcher uses a flywheel-based system to generate launch energy:

  • • A high-speed flywheel stores rotational kinetic energy
  • • A cord mechanism couples the flywheel to the UAV
  • • Energy transfer accelerates the UAV down the launch rail

Analytical Modeling Workbooks

I created comprehensive calculation workbooks to model and predict launcher performance. These analytical models calculated key parameters every 0.01 seconds after launch:

Predicted Parameters

  • Position: Displacement along the launch rail
  • Force: Applied force from the flywheel mechanism
  • Velocity: Velocity profile during acceleration
  • Acceleration: Acceleration as a function of position and time

This high-resolution temporal modeling enabled us to optimize launch parameters and predict system behavior before fabrication and testing.

Design & Manufacturing

Using SolidWorks, I designed, manufactured, and assembled mechanical components for the launcher system:

  • • 3D CAD modeling and engineering drawings
  • • Component fabrication and assembly
  • • Integration of flywheel, cord, and launch rail systems
  • • Collaboration with manufacturing team for production

Field Testing in Huntsville

We conducted live testing of the launcher in collaboration with Griffon Aerospace in Huntsville, Alabama. This testing provided critical validation data:

  • • Comparison of analytical predictions with actual launch data
  • • Optimization of launch parameters based on empirical results
  • • Validation of modeling assumptions and accuracy
  • • Real-world validation in aerospace testing environment

Key Findings

The field testing revealed that while the launch mechanism performed well, the overall structure was not sound enough for the intended application. The structural issues were in the baseline design that existed before my involvement, but this finding led to a complete overhaul of the structural approach.

This experience taught me the value of critical analysis and the importance of questioning baseline assumptions, even when working on established projects.
